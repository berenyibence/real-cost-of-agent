# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

Everyone is welcome here, whatever their experience, background, identity or circumstances. We want
taking part in this project to be a good experience for the people doing it.

## The standard

This project adopts the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1][covenant] as its code of conduct. Read it
there — it is short, and linking it means you get the canonical text rather than someone's paraphrase.

In practice, and specific to this project:

- **Assume good faith about numbers.** This is a tool about money and arithmetic, and being told your
  figure is wrong is not an accusation. "This column does not add up, here is my data" is exactly the
  kind of report we want, and it should be received as a gift rather than as an attack.
- **Nobody is obliged to share their usage.** The share feature exists because some people find their
  number interesting. Pressuring anyone to post theirs, or commenting on what somebody's spend says
  about them, is not on.
- **Reviews are about the change, not the person.** "This double-counts cache writes" is a review.
  "You clearly did not read the code" is not.
- **English is not everyone's first language.** Do not correct someone's grammar in a code review
  unless it is in user-facing prose, and then do it kindly.

Unacceptable behaviour includes harassment, personal attacks, publishing anyone's private information,
sexualised language or imagery, and sustained disruption. Maintainers may edit, hide or remove
contributions that do not meet this standard, and may block accounts that repeat it.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages — and to
anyone representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainers by opening a
[GitHub security advisory][advisory], which is private to you and the maintainers, or by contacting
[@berenyibence](https://github.com/berenyibence) directly on GitHub.

Reports are handled confidentially. You will get an acknowledgement, and you will not be asked to
justify why something bothered you. If a report concerns a maintainer, say so and it will be handled
by someone else where that is possible.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will decide on a response proportionate to what happened, which may be a private word, a
public correction, a temporary block, or a permanent ban. Where a ban is issued, the reason will be
stated to the person concerned.
